Mattering

The Negotiation Room

Two firms' software agents negotiate early payment of an invoice. Each seals its offer so that only the two firms can read it, and only once the step's time comes, so neither agent sees the other's offer before committing its own. Each acts under a mandate whose limit the other side cannot see. Then you check every step yourself.

Don't trust this page

Each step's outcome was worked out in your browser from the evidence and from what the two firms disclosed to you, as they would to an auditor: the keys to both offers and their mandates' limits. Nothing was taken from the server's say-so. Run the checks again whenever you like.

    What you are still trusting

    • The clock.
    • The log's operator to accept every offer submitted. It can refuse one, and nobody could prove it.
    • The cosigners to be independent of the log's operator.
    • The firms' keys. A mandate proves which key signed it, not that the key belongs to the firm named.

    Staged: both agents run on this server, which holds their keys; in practice each runs at its own firm. The firms, the invoice and the limits are invented. Each offer is chosen by a language model when one is configured, and by a script otherwise; each offer says which, and the model's offers are held to the same mandate. An agent's note to its firm is shown once the step has opened; it is not part of the evidence. Mattering is an experimental proof of concept. It has not had a security review.
